Runs entirely in your browser

Solve any Rubik's cube in about twenty moves.

Scramble the cube, or paint in the colours of the one on your desk, and step through a solution. Under the hood is a two-stage search guided by four lookup tables that this page builds the moment it loads; how it works is explained below.

How it works

Two small searches instead of one enormous one

Pattern databases, built on the fly

A breadth-first search from the solved cube records, for every value of a coordinate such as "how the corners are twisted", the exact number of moves needed to fix it. Four such tables, about four million entries in total, are generated by a Web Worker in roughly a second when the page loads. Each one is a lower bound on the moves a full solution needs, which is what lets the search skip almost everything.

IDA*, twice

Phase one uses iterative-deepening A* to orient every piece and bring the four middle-layer edges home, reaching the subgroup where only U, D, R2, L2, F2 and B2 are needed. Phase two finishes the job inside that subgroup. Every phase-one solution is tried in turn, and the shortest total wins: usually twenty or twenty-one moves, most often in a fraction of a second and never longer than the time budget of the setting you chose.

From C++ and Python to the browser

The first version of this solver was Korf's optimal algorithm in C++, driven by three pattern databases with 173 million entries that two Python scripts took hours to build. The cube model, the sticker-to-piece extraction and the IDA* search were ported to JavaScript and restructured so the same idea fits in a browser tab. The README (opens in a new tab) walks through the design and the tests behind it.
